Our Solar System

The Eight Planets

From blazing Mercury to icy Neptune, explore the eight planets that orbit our star, the Sun.

Our Solar Family

The Solar System formed about 4.6 billion years ago. The Sun holds 99.8% of the system's total mass, with the eight planets orbiting it in nearly circular paths.

Mercury

Terrestrial Planet

The smallest planet and closest to the Sun. Mercury experiences extreme temperature swings — from 430 °C during the day to −180 °C at night, because it has almost no atmosphere to retain heat.

Distance from Sun
57.9M km
Day length
59 Earth days

Venus

Terrestrial Planet

Often called Earth's twin due to its similar size, Venus is the hottest planet at 465 °C thanks to a runaway greenhouse effect. It also rotates backwards — a day on Venus lasts longer than its year!

Distance from Sun
108.2M km
Surface temp
465 °C

Earth

Terrestrial Planet

Our home planet is the only known world with life. Liquid water covers 71% of its surface, and a protective magnetic field shields us from harmful solar radiation.

Distance from Sun
149.6M km
Known moons
1

Mars

Terrestrial Planet

The Red Planet has the tallest volcano in the Solar System — Olympus Mons, standing 22 km high. Several rovers are currently exploring its surface searching for signs of ancient life.

Explore Mars in detail → (opens the Mars page)

Jupiter

Gas Giant

The largest planet — more than twice the mass of all other planets combined. Its Great Red Spot is a storm that has lasted for at least 350 years and is wider than Earth.

Known moons
95
Day length
9.9 hrs

Saturn

Gas Giant

Famous for its spectacular ring system made of ice and rock, Saturn is so low-density it could float on water. It has 146 known moons, including Titan with its own thick atmosphere.

Ring width
282,000 km
Known moons
146

Uranus

Ice Giant

Uranus rotates on its side with an axial tilt of 98° — meaning its poles experience 42 years of continuous sunlight followed by 42 years of darkness. It is the coldest planet at −224 °C.

Min temperature
−224 °C
Known moons
28

Neptune

Ice Giant

The most distant planet, Neptune has winds of up to 2,100 km/h — the fastest in the Solar System. One Neptune year lasts 165 Earth years, meaning it completed its first orbit since its discovery in 2011.

Distance from Sun
4.5B km
Year length
165 Earth yrs

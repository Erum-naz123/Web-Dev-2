Explore Mars

A World of Extremes

Mars is roughly half the size of Earth and about a tenth of its mass. Despite this, it is home to the largest volcano in the Solar System, Olympus Mons, standing 22 km above the surrounding plains — nearly three times the height of Mount Everest.

The planet's deep red colour comes from iron oxide — rust — coating its surface. Frequent dust storms can last for months and engulf the entire planet. Temperatures range from a relatively warm 20 °C near the equator in summer to −125 °C at the poles in winter.

Mars has two small, irregularly shaped moons: Phobos and Deimos. Phobos orbits so close to Mars that it will eventually crash into the planet or break apart in about 50 million years.

Major Geographical Features

🌋

Olympus Mons

The largest volcano in the Solar System at 22 km high and 600 km wide — roughly the size of France. It is a shield volcano built up by countless lava flows over billions of years.

🏔️

Valles Marineris

A canyon system stretching 4,000 km in length and up to 7 km deep — dwarfing the Grand Canyon. It was likely formed by tectonic activity and erosion billions of years ago.

❄️

Polar Ice Caps

Both poles have permanent ice caps made of water ice and dry ice (frozen CO₂). The northern polar cap can span up to 1,000 km across in winter.

🌊

Ancient Riverbeds

Channels and valley networks across the surface suggest that liquid water once flowed on Mars over 3 billion years ago. Today, liquid water cannot exist on the surface due to the low atmospheric pressure.

Key Mars Missions

🚀

Mariner 4 (1965)

The first spacecraft to fly past Mars and return close-up images, revealing a cratered, Moon-like surface and a thin atmosphere.

🛸

Viking 1 & 2 (1976)

NASA's Viking landers were the first to successfully land on Mars and conduct experiments. They searched for signs of life but returned inconclusive results.

🤖

Curiosity Rover (2012–present)

Still operational today, Curiosity is a car-sized rover exploring Gale Crater. It confirmed that Mars once had the right conditions for microbial life.

🚁

Perseverance & Ingenuity (2021–present)

Perseverance is collecting rock samples for eventual return to Earth. Ingenuity — the first powered aircraft to fly on another planet — completed over 70 flights before retiring in 2024.

Could Life Have Existed on Mars?

Scientists believe that Mars may have supported microbial life billions of years ago, when the planet was warmer and wetter. The Curiosity rover discovered organic molecules — the chemical building blocks of life — in ancient rock sediments.

In 2023, the Perseverance rover found carbon-rich organic compounds in Jezero Crater — an ancient lake bed — one of the most promising signs yet that life may once have existed on Mars.

While no definitive proof of life has been found, the Mars Sample Return mission — planned for the 2030s — aims to bring Martian rocks back to Earth for detailed laboratory analysis. The answer may lie in those samples.
